## How to use this

The scripts are a spine, not a recital. Say them in your own words, in the
language the buyer is comfortable in. What must not change is the **order**,
the **numbers**, and the **things we don't say**.

Three rules that override anything else in this document:

1. **Never quote a possession date.** Phase 1 is completed — that is a strong,
   true fact and it is the one you use. Phase 2's date is not confirmed, so we
   do not have one to give. Saying "I'll get you the exact RERA date in
   writing" is a better answer than a guess, and it buys you a follow-up.
2. **Never promise a return, rent, or appreciation.** Not in writing, not on a
   call, not "off the record" in the car park. If a buyer asks what it will be
   worth in five years, the answer is in Objection 9.
3. **Never invent scarcity.** No "two units left" unless {{AVAILABILITY}} says
   two units are left, that day.

# 1. The five qualification questions

Ask in this order. The order matters — budget before timeline makes people
defensive; configuration first makes it a conversation about their family.

### Q1. "Which one were you looking at — the 2 BHK or the 3 BHK?"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Good answer** | "3 BHK, we're four of us and my parents visit." — they've pictured living in it. |
| **Bad answer** | "Whatever is cheapest." — they're shopping price across developers, not choosing a home. Not a disqualification, but slow down and find out what they actually need. |
| **Then ask** | "Who's going to live there with you?" It tells you the real configuration and who else is in the decision. |

### Q2. "What budget are you comfortable in, all-in — including registration?"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Good answer** | A number, or a range, given without hesitation. Even a low one. |
| **Bad answer** | "You tell me your price first." — normal, not hostile. Answer with: "Our range is {{SG4_PRICE}}. Does that sit inside what you had in mind, honestly?" |
| **Watch for** | "All-in" is doing work here. Many buyers budget the flat and forget registration, and that gap kills deals at the last step. Surface it now, not at booking. |

### Q3. "When are you hoping to move in?"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Good answer** | "Before my daughter's school year starts in June." — a real-world date with a reason behind it. |
| **Bad answer** | "No hurry, just looking." — park them in a long-cycle follow-up, don't burn the team's week on them. Still worth a site visit; not worth six calls. |
| **Careful** | If they need to move in within months, Phase 1 completed is the entire conversation. If they're two years out, don't overpromise Phase 2 — see rule 1. |

### Q4. "Home loan, or self-funded?"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Good answer** | "Loan — I'm at [employer], eight years." Salaried with tenure is the cleanest file. |
| **Bad answer** | "I'll manage it somehow." — the deal will die at sanction. Get to it now: "Shall we get a pre-approval started this week? Costs nothing and you'll know where you stand." |
| **Ask next** | "Have you checked if you qualify for PMAY?" — and stop there. **Do not offer help with the application until SG4 eligibility is confirmed.** We have the award on SG2; we do not yet have the answer for SG4. [FILL] |

### Q5. "Apart from you, who else needs to say yes?"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Good answer** | "My wife and my father." — now you know to get all three to the site, once. |
| **Bad answer** | "Only me." said by someone who then goes quiet for ten days. Nobody buys a first home alone. Ask gently a second way: "Will anyone come with you to see it?" |
| **The rule** | Never run a full site visit for a person who cannot say yes. Run a short one, then book the real visit with the decision-maker present. |

### Disqualify honestly

If the budget is well below our entry price and they cannot stretch, tell them
kindly and let them go. "Honestly, we'd be stretching you, and that's not a
good way to buy your first home. If your budget moves, call me directly."
People remember being told the truth and they send their cousin.

# 2. The site visit itself

**Target: 45 minutes. Order is fixed.**

### Stop 1 — The bus stand, before you enter the gate (2 min)

Start outside. Stand where they can see the bus stand.

> "Before we go in — that's the bus stand. One, two minutes walking. Honaga
> industrial area is five to ten minutes that way. Whatever else you decide
> today, you won't find that combination easily in Belagavi."

**Why first:** location is our strongest unarguable fact and it costs nothing
to prove. It also frames everything after it.

### Stop 2 — The completed Phase 1 blocks (10 min)

Walk them past occupied homes. Lights on, cycles outside, people.

> "Phase 1 is complete. Families are living here now. You're not looking at a
> plan — you're looking at the thing."

Say the track record once, plainly, and then stop saying it:

> "We've delivered about 1,560 homes across Tumkur, Kolar and Belagavi. Our
> Tumkur project won a PMAY award. In Kolar we built the town's first
> apartment community. We finish what we start."

### Stop 3 — The community spaces (8 min)

Play area, shared spaces, parking, security gate. Let them see other residents.

> "This is what most people in Belagavi are actually buying when they move out
> of an independent house — not the extra room, the gate and the people."

### Stop 4 — Inside the unit (15 min)

Let them walk in first and say nothing for thirty seconds. Let them look.

Then the home automation, demonstrated, not described — lights, lock, comfort
settings on the app.

**The question to ask while they are standing in the unit:**

> "Where would your daughter study?"

Or the version that fits their family — where the parents sleep when they
visit, where the pooja shelf goes, where he'd keep the bike. Ask them to place
one real thing from their life into the room. The buyer who answers that
question has already moved in, in their head. The buyer who shrugs is not
close, and you now know it while you can still do something about it.

### Stop 5 — Sit down (10 min)

Water, chair, shade. This is where you ask for the decision, not in the lift.

> "What did you think, honestly?"
> ... let them talk ...
> "What would have to be true for you to book this?"

That second question is the most valuable sentence in this document. The answer
is your entire follow-up plan.

### The one thing you leave unsaid

**Do not show the highest-floor unit or the best view on visit one.** Mention
that higher-floor units exist, don't take them up.

> "There's a better view three floors up. I'd rather show you that one when
> you come back with your wife — it's worth seeing properly."

That is a genuine, honest reason for a second visit, and second visits are
where SG4 gets booked. It is not a trick: the view really is better and it
really is worth a dedicated look.

# 3. The follow-up sequence

Every touch carries a new reason to talk. **Never "just checking in."** If you
have nothing new to say, you have nothing to send — find something real.

Response-time standard: first WhatsApp within **5 minutes** of the enquiry,
call within **30**. No lead sits untouched past 24 hours. Ever.

## Day 0 — the evening of the visit (or the hour of the enquiry)

This is the touch that changes by lead source. Everything from Day 2 onward is
common, with one variation noted.

### → Paid lead (Meta / Google / portal)

They filled a form. They may not remember doing it. Do not open with "Thank
you for your interest in Swarna Griha 4."

> Hi [name], this is [you] from Swarna Griha. You asked about our Belagavi
> project — Swarna Griha 4 at Kakati.
>
> One thing that usually decides it for people: Phase 1 is already complete and
> families are living there. It's not a launch.
>
> Two quick questions so I don't waste your time — 2 BHK or 3 BHK, and are you
> looking to move soon or planning ahead?
>
> — [you], Swarna Griha
> RERA PRM/KA/RERA/1249/447/PR/280222/004737

**Then call within 30 minutes.** The WhatsApp is to make the call expected, not
to replace it.

### → Walk-in at the Kakati site

They came to us. That's the strongest signal in the business. Match it.

> [name], good to meet you today. As promised — the floor plan for the [2/3]
> BHK, and a photo of the block you liked.
>
> You asked about [the exact thing they asked about]. Here's the honest answer:
> [answer].
>
> I'm keeping [Saturday] morning free if you want to bring [family member] and
> see the higher floor.

Send within two hours of them leaving the gate, while the place is still in
their head.

### → Referral from an existing resident

Lead with the person who sent them. That name is worth more than any brochure.

> [name], [referrer] said you might call — good to hear from you.
>
> They've been in [SG1 / SG2 / Serene Highlands] since [year]. Ask them
> anything you like about us; that's the most honest review you'll get.
>
> For Belagavi — Swarna Griha 4 at Kakati, Phase 1 complete and occupied.
> When can you come and see it? I'll keep time aside properly.

Never oversell to a referral. The referrer already did the selling and heavy
pitching makes both of them suspicious.

### → Channel partner lead

Two messages — one to the partner, one to the buyer. Never let the buyer feel
handed over like a file.

**To the partner:**
> [partner name] — got [buyer]. Calling them in 10 minutes. Site visit slots
> open [days]. I'll keep you copied on where it reaches.

**To the buyer:**
> Hi [name], [partner] connected us — I'm [you] from Swarna Griha, at the
> Kakati project directly.
>
> Anything you want to check about the project, the approvals or the paperwork,
> ask me straight and I'll give you the actual answer.
>
> Before we fix a visit: 2 BHK or 3 BHK, and what's your timeline?

## Day 2 — the document

**Reason to talk:** something in writing they can forward to whoever else
decides.

> [name], sending the brochure and the floor plan for the [2/3] BHK.
>
> RERA number is on it — PRM/KA/RERA/1249/447/PR/280222/004737. You can check
> it yourself on the Karnataka RERA site, and I'd encourage you to.
>
> Show it to [family member they named in Q5] and tell me what they ask. Their
> questions are usually the ones that matter.

*Referral variation:* add — "[referrer] can tell you what the handover was
actually like. Worth a call."

**If they have not yet visited**, replace the last line with:
> Which is easier for you — Saturday morning or Sunday evening?

Two options, not "when are you free". Open questions get no reply.

## Day 5 — the proof

**Reason to talk:** evidence, not persuasion. Pick the one that fits what they
worried about on Day 0.

- Worried about the developer → photos of completed, occupied Phase 1
- Worried about the location → the bus stand and Honaga, with the drive time
- Worried about the loan → bank list and what documents a sanction needs
  [FILL: approved bank list]
- Worried about the ground / water table → the engineer's foundation note
  [FILL: get the engineer's one-pager — see Objection 4]

> [name], you'd asked about [their worry]. Rather than tell you it's fine,
> here's [the document / the photos].
>
> Read it and call me if anything doesn't add up. I'd rather answer it now
> than after you've booked.

## Day 10 — the decision-maker

By now you know whether the person you're talking to can say yes.

**If someone else decides:**
> [name], easiest thing now is 20 minutes with [spouse / father / brother] on
> site. They'll have questions I can answer on the spot that don't work over
> the phone.
>
> Saturday 11, or Sunday 5?

**If they can decide and haven't:**
> [name], straight question — what's holding it? Price, timing, or the
> location?
>
> Tell me which one and I'll either fix it or tell you honestly that I can't.
> Either way you'll know where you stand.

That message works because it is not a nudge. It asks for a real answer and it
offers a real outcome. Most buyers reply to it.

## Day 21 — the honest close of the loop

Not a last-chance message. We don't run those.

> [name], I'll stop messaging after this so I'm not a nuisance.
>
> Swarna Griha 4 is there when you're ready — Phase 1 is complete, families are
> living in it, and it's not going anywhere.
>
> If your plans change, or if the budget shifts, message me directly and I'll
> pick it up from here. And if you've bought somewhere else — genuinely, well
> done. Buying a first home is a big thing.

**Then actually stop**, and move them to the quarterly revival list. This
message brings back more buyers at month four than five more chase calls do,
and it is the only ethical way to end a sequence.

## After Day 21 — the revival list

Message once a quarter, only with real news: a new phase, a completed
milestone, a bank added, a genuine price revision. Never a manufactured reason.
Broadcast copy for these goes through the WhatsApp skill so the list stays
compliant.

# 4. Objection handling

> **Owner: read this.** `business.md` lists "Why buyers say no" as still to be
> filled. The objections below are the ones this market and this project
> reliably produce, plus SG4's known site history. **Get the sales team's exact
> words** and I'll rewrite this section with the real ones — their phrasing
> beats my prediction every time.

The rule for all of them: **never deny something true.** Acknowledge it, then
reframe against what we genuinely do better. A salesperson who concedes one
real point is believed on the next ten.

### Objection 1 — "It's too expensive."

- **Actually worried about:** the EMI landing on top of rent they're already
  paying, and looking foolish to their family if it goes wrong.
- **Say:** "Tell me the number you're comfortable with. If we're above it, I'll
  say so — I'd rather you hear that from me than stretch and regret it."
- **Show:** the payment schedule, broken into what's due when. Most "too
  expensive" is actually "too much, too soon."
- **Move it forward:** "If the payments were spread differently, does the total
  work?" — that separates a price problem from a cash-flow problem. They need
  completely different solutions.
- **Never:** compute an EMI from a number you don't have. Pricing is
  {{SG4_PRICE}} and nothing else.

### Objection 2 — "The project next door is cheaper."

- **Actually worried about:** overpaying. Not about the other project.
- **Say:** "It might well be. Cheaper per square foot isn't the same as cheaper
  to own — ask them what's carpet and what's built-up, what the maintenance is,
  and when they last handed over a completed building."
- **Show:** Phase 1, occupied. 1,560 homes delivered. A PMAY award.
- **Move it forward:** "Go and see it. Seriously. Then come back and tell me
  what you found — if they're better for you, I'll say so."
- **Never:** rubbish a competitor by name. It makes us look small and buyers
  tell each other.

### Objection 3 — "I'd rather buy a site and build my own house."

- **Actually worried about:** land feels safer and more permanent than a flat.
  In tier-2 Karnataka this belief is deep and it is not stupid.
- **Say:** "A lot of people here feel that, and building your own house is a
  real option. The honest difference is what happens at 9pm when you're not
  home — the gate, the security, the neighbours, the lift for your parents.
  That's what people are buying here."
- **Show:** the community at Phase 1 in the evening. Take them at the right
  time of day if you can.
- **Move it forward:** "Who's at home during the day when you're at work?"
- **Never:** claim a flat appreciates better than land. We make no appreciation
  claims of any kind.

### Objection 4 — "I heard the land here was low-lying / there's a water problem."

**This one is true and it must be handled honestly. Never deny it.**

- **Actually worried about:** the building being unsafe, or a lifetime of
  seepage and repairs.
- **Say:** "You've heard right — the site sat in a natural depression with a
  high water table. That's exactly why the foundation was engineered for it,
  with dewatering during construction. It was designed for that ground, not
  in spite of it."
- **Show:** the engineer's foundation note [FILL: get this one-pager — it is
  the single highest-value missing document in this entire handbook], and
  Phase 1, standing and occupied through monsoons.
- **Move it forward:** "Come during or after a rain, and look at the drainage
  yourself. I'd rather you saw it than took my word."
- **Why honesty wins here:** the buyer has already heard the rumour. If you
  dodge, you confirm it. If you explain the engineering, you become the only
  person who told them the truth — and every other objection gets easier.

### Objection 5 — "When is possession?"

- **Actually worried about:** paying an EMI and rent at the same time for years.
- **Say (Phase 1):** "Phase 1 is complete. Families are living in it now."
- **Say (Phase 2):** "I'm not going to give you a date I can't stand behind. I'll
  get you the committed RERA date in writing and send it across." Then get it —
  {{POSSESSION_DATE}} — and send it.
- **Show:** the completed blocks. Completion beats any promised date.
- **Move it forward:** "Are you paying rent right now? Then Phase 1 is the part
  of this you should be looking at."
- **Never:** name a month. Not "around December", not "by next Diwali."

### Objection 6 — "Will I be able to sell it later? It's Belagavi."

- **Actually worried about:** being trapped in an illiquid asset in a small city.
- **Say:** "Fair question. What I can tell you is factual, not a forecast:
  Honaga industrial area is five to ten minutes away, the bus stand is two
  minutes, and our three earlier projects sold out completely. What that means
  for a resale price in ten years, nobody can tell you — and anyone who does is
  making it up."
- **Show:** sold-out status of SG1, SG2 and Serene Highlands. Facts, not
  forecasts.
- **Move it forward:** "Are you buying this to live in, or to hold?" The answer
  changes the whole conversation, and we're better for the first one.
- **Never, under any circumstances:** a percentage, a doubling, a rental yield,
  an "investment will grow" — verbally or in writing. This is the line that
  gets a developer prosecuted, not just embarrassed.

### Objection 7 — "Let me discuss with my family."

- **Actually worried about:** usually a real second decision-maker. Sometimes a
  polite exit.
- **Say:** "Of course. What do you think they'll ask that I haven't answered?"
- **Show:** nothing. Listen.
- **Move it forward:** "Bring them Saturday — it'll take you one visit instead
  of three phone calls." Get a date before the conversation ends.
- **Diagnostic:** if they won't name the family member or fix a date, this is a
  soft no. Respect it, move them to Day 21, and spend your week on someone real.

### Objection 8 — "I'm not sure my loan will come through."

- **Actually worried about:** the humiliation of being rejected.
- **Say:** "Let's find out this week instead of worrying about it for a month.
  A pre-approval costs nothing and doesn't commit you to anything."
- **Show:** the approved bank list [FILL], the document checklist, and PMAY
  guidance if eligible [FILL: confirm SG4 PMAY eligibility].
- **Move it forward:** "Salaried or business? I'll tell you which of our banks
  is easiest for your profile."
- **Never:** promise a sanction, an interest rate, or a subsidy amount.

### Objection 9 — "I'll wait. Prices might come down."

- **Actually worried about:** buying at the wrong moment.
- **Say:** "I can't tell you what prices will do — nobody can. What I can tell
  you is what's true today: what's available, and at what price." Then stop.
  Don't fill the silence with a forecast.
- **Show:** {{AVAILABILITY}} — only if it is the real, current number.
- **Move it forward:** "Is it the price you're waiting on, or something else?"
- **Never:** "prices are going up next month" unless a revision is actually
  signed off and dated. A fake deadline is the fastest way to lose a referral
  network in a city this size.

### Objection 10 — "Twelve floors is too tall for me."

- **Actually worried about:** lifts failing, water pressure, elderly parents,
  fire.
- **Say:** "What's the real worry — the lift, or your parents?" Then answer the
  actual one.
- **Show:** power backup, the lifts running in occupied Phase 1, lower-floor
  options.
- **Move it forward:** "Let me show you a lower floor. Same home, different
  answer."

in this document creates one. If a deal needs a price concession, it goes to
the owner, by name, with the reason — and the buyer is told "I need to ask,
I'll have an answer by [time]", never "I'll see what I can do."

## Give things away in this order — never skip ahead

| Order | What we can flex | What it costs us | Say it like this |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Payment schedule staging** — when instalments fall, not the total | Almost nothing | "I can move the second instalment out. Would that make this work?" |
| 2 | **Floor rise** — waive or reduce on selected floors [FILL: which floors, what authority] | Small | "Let me see if I can take the floor rise off this one." |
| 3 | **Parking** — allocation and position, covered where available [FILL: what's available] | Small, finite | "I can get you a better-placed parking. That one I can do." |
| 4 | **Registration timing** — when, not whether | Nothing, if paperwork allows | "We can schedule registration to suit your funds coming in." |
| 5 | **Handover support** — bank coordination and documentation help. **PMAY guidance only once SG4 eligibility is confirmed** [FILL] | Time only | Offer the bank and document help freely and early. Hold the PMAY line until it is confirmed. |
| 6 | **Price** | — | **Owner approval only. Every time.** |

## What we do not flex, ever

- **The RERA-committed specification.** Not a fitting, not a material, not a
  layout. What's registered is what's built.
- **Payment before the agreement.** No cash-in-hand "blocking" outside the
  process.
- **The carpet / built-up figure.** Say the area exactly as it is defined and
  never blur it to close a deal. [FILL: confirm whether ~950 sq ft is carpet or
  super built-up — until it's confirmed, say "approximately 950 square feet"
  and nothing more. See {{AREA_BASIS}}.]
- **Anything given verbally and not written into the agreement.** If you
  promise it, it goes in the paperwork. If it can't go in the paperwork, don't
  promise it.

## Three rules of the room

1. **Never give a concession they didn't ask for.** You've spent it for nothing.
2. **Every concession buys something.** "I can do that — can you book today?"
   A free concession teaches them there are more.
3. **Silence after your offer.** Make it, then stop talking. The next person
   who speaks concedes.
